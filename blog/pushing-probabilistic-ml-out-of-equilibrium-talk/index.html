The problem · what we ask of models

We ask models for samples and estimations

Samples: a drug candidate, a conformation, a structure
Estimations: a free energy, a phase boundary, a rate
A learned model is always imperfect, so both need debiasing.
The problem · the fix

Debias by reweighting

$\mathbb E_\pi[f]=\mathbb E_{x\sim p_\theta}\!\Big[\underbrace{\textcolor{#E76F51}{\dfrac{\pi(x)}{p_\theta(x)}}}_{\textcolor{#E76F51}{\text{weight } w(x)}}\,f(x)\Big]$
Exact for any model; its quality only sets the variance.
With an exact density, all one line
Question$w=\tilde\pi/p_\theta$
Expectations$\mathbb E[wf]/\mathbb E[w]$
Free energy$Z=\mathbb E[w]$
Samplesresample $\propto w$
Steeringweight by $h(x)$
Exact densities are valuable, but expensive for expressive models.
The problem · why it is hard

Why it is hard: expressive, cheap, or tractable, pick two

$\mathbb E_\pi[f]=\mathbb E_{x\sim p_\theta}\!\Big[\dfrac{\pi(x)}{\textcolor{#E76F51}{p_\theta(x)}}\,f(x)\Big]$
Reweighting needs the model's likelihood $p_\theta(x)$, for every sample. Which models can give it cheaply?
MCMC cost from time
Normalizing flows cost from architecture
Continuous flows cost from Jacobians
Diffusion models change the space
on paths, density costs as much as sampling, and an unbiased estimate is all reweighting needs

Two processes through the same marginals $p_t$:
$\begin{aligned}\Pf\ \text{(generation, }0\to N)&:\quad \mathrm dX_t=\mu^+_t(X_t)\,\mathrm dt+\sigma_t\,\mathrm dW_t\\[4pt] \Pb\ \text{(noising, }N\to 0)&:\quad \mathrm dX_t=\mu^-_t(X_t)\,\mathrm dt+\sigma_t\,\mathrm d\overleftarrow{W}_t\end{aligned}$
Time reversal: their drifts differ by the score, the only unknown
$\mu^+_t-\mu^-_t=\sigma_t^2\,\textcolor{#E76F51}{\nabla\log p_t}$
Fokker–Planck: move the diffusion term into the drift
$\partial_tp_t=-\nabla\!\cdot(\mu^+_tp_t)+\tfrac12\sigma_t^2\Delta p_t=-\nabla\!\cdot\big((\mu^+_t-\tfrac12\sigma_t^2\textcolor{#E76F51}{\nabla\log p_t})\,p_t\big)$
so the ODE $\dot x=\mu^+_t-\tfrac12\sigma_t^2\textcolor{#E76F51}{\nabla\log p_t}$ has the same marginals: flows and diffusions are equivalent up to the score.
Learning a diffusion (or a flow) = learning the score at every noise level.
Sohl-Dickstein et al. 2015 · Ho et al. 2020 · Song et al. 2021 · Nelson 1967, Anderson 1982 · Lipman et al. 2023, Albergo et al. 2023 (flow matching, interpolants)
Path space · the path weight

Path weights: time reversal is Bayes' rule in time

$p_t(X_t)\,\Pf(X_{t+1}\mid X_t)=p_{t+1}(X_{t+1})\,\Pb(X_t\mid X_{t+1})$
⟹
$\dfrac{p_N(X_N)}{p_0(X_0)}=\dfrac{\prod_t\Pf(X_{t+1}\mid X_t)}{\prod_t\Pb(X_t\mid X_{t+1})}$
intractable marginals = a ratio of tractable kernels: one network call per step, no Jacobians
Path weight $\;w(X_{0:N})=\dfrac{\tilde\pi(X_N)\prod_t\Pb(X_t\mid X_{t+1})}{p_0(X_0)\prod_t\Pf(X_{t+1}\mid X_t)}\;$ computable for any model; $W=-\log w$ is the work.
He, Hernández-Lobato, Du†, Vargas†, ICLR 2026 (RNE) · Crooks 1999 · right: a real path of the toy sampler with its kernels
Path space · the physics

The path weight is the work

Jarzynski
$\mathbb E_{\Pf}\big[e^{-W}\big]=e^{-\Delta F}$
= annealed importance sampling
Crooks
$\dfrac{\dd\Pb}{\dd\Pf}=e^{-(W-\Delta F)}$
= time reversal
Second law
$\langle W\rangle\ge\Delta F$
= the ELBO
Zero variance $\iff$ reversible in finite time $\iff$ a perfect diffusion model.

Inference · inference-time scaling

One weight, two algorithms: SMC and replica exchange

Work of one step
$W_t=-\log\dfrac{q_{t+1}(x_{t+1})\,\Pb(x_t\mid x_{t+1})}{q_t(x_t)\,\Pf(x_{t+1}\mid x_t)}$
SMC: resample with
$w^i\propto e^{-\sum_{s\le t}W_s^i}$
Replica exchange: accept a swap with
$\min\big\{1,\ e^{-W_t^{\uparrow}+W_t^{\downarrow}}\big\}$
↑ replica moved up a level, ↓ moved down
More particles or rounds, more accuracy: inference-time scaling, with an unbiased $Z$ from both.

Training · from data

With data: denoising score matching

Noise a data point with the backward kernel:
$X_t\sim\Pb(\cdot\mid X_N)=\mathcal N\big(\alpha_tX_N,\ s_t^2I\big),\qquad X_N\sim\pi\ \ (\text{data})$
Bayes' rule over the clean point gives the noised score:
$\nabla\log p_t(x_t)=\mathbb E_{\pi(x_N\mid x_t)}\big[\nabla_{x_t}\log\Pb(x_t\mid X_N)\big]=\mathbb E\Big[-\dfrac{X_t-\alpha_tX_N}{s_t^2}\,\Big|\,X_t=x_t\Big]$
Regress the score network onto that label, the direction back to the clean point:
$\mathcal L_{\rm DSM}(\theta)=\displaystyle\int_0^1\!\lambda_t\;\mathbb E\,\Big\|\,s_\theta(X_t,t)+\frac{X_t-\alpha_tX_N}{s_t^2}\Big\|^2\dd t$
Why this loss? It maximizes a bound on the likelihood (the ELBO):
$\mathrm{KL}(\pi\,\|\,p_\theta)\le\mathrm{KL}\big(\Pb\,\|\,\Pf_\theta\big)=\tfrac12\displaystyle\int_0^1\!\sigma_t^2\;\mathbb E\,\big\|s_\theta(X_t,t)-\nabla\log p_t(X_t)\big\|^2\dd t$
$-\mathbb E_\pi\log p_\theta=\mathrm{KL}(\pi\,\|\,p_\theta)+{\rm const}$; the path KL equals $\mathcal L_{\rm DSM}$ with $\lambda_t=\sigma_t^2/2$, up to a $\theta$-free constant
Its minimizer is the score $\nabla\log p_t$: plain regression, so it scales.
But every label needs a clean sample $X_N\sim\pi$: exactly what we lack for a Boltzmann distribution.
Hyvärinen 2005 · Vincent 2011 (denoising score identity) · Ho et al. 2020 · Song et al. 2021 · Song, Durkan et al. 2021 (likelihood) · left: exact score of the noised mixture ($\alpha_t=1$)
Training · from energy

Without data: target score matching

Same noising kernel; integrate by parts to move the gradient onto the target:
$\nabla\log p_t(x_t)=\dfrac{1}{\alpha_t}\,\mathbb E_{\pi(x_N\mid x_t)}\big[\nabla\log\tilde\pi(X_N)\big]=\dfrac{1}{\alpha_t}\,\mathbb E\big[\beta\mathbf F(X_N)\,\big|\,X_t=x_t\big]$
the noised score is the posterior-averaged force; $Z$ never appears
$\mathcal L_{\rm TSM}(\theta)=\displaystyle\int_0^1\!\lambda_t\;\mathbb E\,\Big\|\,s_\theta(X_t,t)-\frac{1}{\alpha_t}\,\beta\mathbf F(X_N)\Big\|^2\dd t$
① data → forces: physics enters through $\mathbf F=-\nabla U$, one force call per sample.
② but the expectation is still over $X_N\sim\pi$, the very thing we want to learn.
The identity changes the representation, from data samples to the target score (forces), but on its own it does not give us a learning algorithm.
De Bortoli et al. 2024 (target score matching) · Blessing et al., ICML 2026
Training · the fixed-point loop

From energy: the model proposes, the physics corrects

$s^{(k+1)}=\arg\min_s\displaystyle\int_0^1\!\lambda_t\;\mathbb E_{X_{0:N}\sim\Pf_{s^{(k)}}}\Big[\textcolor{#E76F51}{\bar w}\;\Big\|\,s(X'_t,t)-\frac{1}{\alpha_t}\beta\mathbf F(X_N)\Big\|^2\Big]\dd t,\qquad X'_t=\alpha_tX_N+s_t\varepsilon$
$\bar w$: the self-normalized path weight, the same weight we used for inference
Where the cost goes
① Generate: expensive, a full rollout of the model
② Label: expensive, one force call per sample, the only oracle calls
③ Interpolate: cheap, fresh noise, no oracle
④ Regress: cheap, plain regression
Why a buffer: pay ① and ② once per sample, then reuse every buffered sample for many cheap rounds of ③ and ④.
The buffer turns two expensive steps into a one-off cost per sample; training is cheap regression.
Blessing et al., ICML 2026 (Bridge Matching Sampler) · Havens et al. 2025 (Adjoint Sampling)

Any Markov process: the path weight only needs kernels

$w(X_{0:N})=\dfrac{\tilde\pi(X_N)\,\prod_t\Pb(X_t\mid X_{t+1})}{p_0(X_0)\,\prod_t\Pf(X_{t+1}\mid X_t)}$

the state is a trajectory

Any state space · language models

Autoregressive language models are non-equilibrium processes too

Forward kernel generate
$\Pf_i(x_{0:i}\mid x_{0:i-1})=p(x_i\mid x_{\lt i})$
Backward kernel trivial
$\Pb_{i-1}=\dfrac{p_{i-1}\,\Pf_i}{p_i}=1$ (delete the last token)
So the path weight exact
$=\displaystyle\prod_i p(x_i\mid x_{\lt i})=p(x)$
Every tool here applies: steer to $p^\beta$, $p\,e^{r}$ or $p\,p'$ with SMC and replica exchange, and quality scales with inference compute, no reward model needed.

Annealing
$q_\lambda\propto p_0^{\,1-\lambda}\,\pi^{\lambda}$

Backup · training: related work

How do we train it? How the field got here

2018 · Boltzmann generators
A normalizing flow: exact likelihood $p_\theta(x)$, so reweighting to $\pi$ is exact.
But invertible layers limit expressiveness, and it is hard to scale to large, flexible systems.
2022–24 · a divergence on whole paths
$\mathrm{KL}\big(\Pf_\theta\,\big\|\,\Pb\big)=\mathbb E_{\textcolor{#E76F51}{X_{0:N}\sim\Pf_\theta}}\Big[\log\dfrac{\mathrm d\Pf_\theta}{\mathrm d\Pb}(X_{0:N})\Big]$
$\text{or}\ \ \mathrm{Var}_{X_{0:N}\sim\mathbb Q}\Big[\log\dfrac{\mathrm d\Pb}{\mathrm d\Pf_\theta}(X_{0:N})\Big]$
The reverse KL is evaluated on paths sampled from the model itself; the log ratio is the path weight (from earlier). But: backprop through the solver, no reuse of energy calls, mode collapse.
2024 · PDDS → iDEM: the right idea
Regress the score on the model's own samples; iDEM adds the replay buffer. Each step closer to the final loop.
But the debiasing sits inside the label: SMC weights (PDDS), an importance-sampled score $\nabla\log\frac1K\sum_k e^{-\beta U(x+s_t\varepsilon_k)}$ (iDEM). High variance → fails at scale.
2025–26 · fixed-point matching
Same loop, but the label is one force $\beta\mathbf F(X_N)$ at the model's own endpoint: no weights inside the regression, simulation-free, replay buffer.
Regression shapes each mode; it does not fix the mode weights.
ATLAS · JANUS = fixed-point matching + the 2022–24 path weight loss, back as a regularizer for the mode weights.
