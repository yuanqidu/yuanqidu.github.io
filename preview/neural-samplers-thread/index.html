How do we sample the equilibrium states of matter? 🧵 Glasses, alloys, surfaces: their properties are set by equilibrium states, and sampling them is extremely hard, as energy barriers trap simulations. Neural samplers learn them from the energy: a new era of simulating matter.

Yuanqi Du @YuanqiD · 3/ why sampling is hard 279
Why is this hard? Configurations follow p(x) ∝ exp(−U/k_BT), explored by local MD or Monte Carlo moves. A barrier ΔU costs ~exp(ΔU/k_BT) steps: in a glass or between phases the chain stays in one basin. ML potentials cut the cost per energy evaluation, not the number needed. ⏳
Yuanqi Du @YuanqiD · 4/ neural samplers 268
Neural samplers learn the sampler instead: a diffusion process from a simple prior to the Boltzmann distribution, trained from energies and forces. Samples are independent, one model spans many conditions, and path weights give unbiased averages and free energies. 🧠
Yuanqi Du @YuanqiD · 5/ ATLAS 275
ATLAS samples amorphous materials. One temperature-conditioned model generates a 2D glass former from the liquid down to the glass, where Monte Carlo leaves every atom caged. Structure matches parallel tempering; the free energy is within 0.2% at >500× fewer evaluations. 🧊
Yuanqi Du @YuanqiD · 6/ ATLAS 277
ATLAS is amortized over composition: one 36 GPU-h pretraining instead of ~20 GPU-h per composition, ~300× less compute over 480 compositions. An LLM agent calls it as a tool and finds the stiffness–ductility Pareto front of high-entropy metallic glasses in 480 evaluations. 🤖
Yuanqi Du @YuanqiD · 7/ JANUS 275
JANUS samples chemical disorder: discrete species per site plus continuous displacements and volume, generated jointly by masked discrete + continuous diffusion, conditioned on T and Δμ. Free energies give phase diagrams (Cu–Ag, Ni–Cr) at ~10³× fewer evaluations than MC. ⚖️
Yuanqi Du @YuanqiD · 8/ JANUS 278
JANUS also runs grand-canonically on a lattice: 15 dopant elements and vacancies in Si and diamond. Motifs over-represented in 8,000 samples go to hybrid DFT: the Si E centre is recovered, and S–Ti (Si) and B–[O]–O (diamond) appear as new candidates with deep in-gap states. 💎
Yuanqi Du @YuanqiD · 9/ OMNIA 272
OMNIA goes off-lattice and opens the system to a reservoir: one model per metal, combined by free energies, gives H coverage on Pt(111) and Pd(111) at every T and μ_H. Pt saturates near a monolayer; Pd takes H below the surface, as in GCMC, at 7–11× fewer evaluations. ⚗️
Yuanqi Du @YuanqiD · 10/ OMNIA 262
Sodium in hard carbon, a sodium-ion battery anode. One OMNIA model is conditioned on the carbon framework: trained on 8,032 frameworks (1.0–2.0 g/cm³), tested on held-out ones. Bound Na peaks near 1.6 g/cm³ as in GCMC, at 38× fewer evaluations per framework. 🔋
Yuanqi Du @YuanqiD · 11/ method 274
No equilibrium data needed. The model proposes, the physics corrects: generate samples, label each with one force call, re-noise along an interpolant, regress the score on the force. The Boltzmann distribution is the fixed point; a replay buffer reuses every force call. 🔁
Yuanqi Du @YuanqiD · 12/ method 275
Each generated path carries a weight, the ratio of backward to forward path densities; −log w is the work (Jarzynski). Path weights fix the mass of each mode, give free energies Z = E[w], and steer the sampler to a tilted target (e.g. a property) with SMC, no retraining. 🎯
Yuanqi Du @YuanqiD · 13/ method 261
OMNIA writes every ensemble as p(x,z) = p(x|z) w(z): canonical samplers at fixed volume, composition or atom number, weighted by free energy. NVT, NPT, ΔμNPT, μVT on and off lattice. Next to universal ML potentials, a step toward foundation neural samplers. 🚀
