Yuanqi Du @YuanqiD · 1/ hook 608
How fast do rare events happen, and through which mechanism? 🧵 Pharos answers this at scale: 🧬 realistic systems: 10,000+ atoms in explicit water 🧂 transferable models, toward foundation models of rare events ⚗️ chemical reactions with ML potentials Folding, binding, reactions are decided by rare transitions that plain MD rarely sees: one crossing can take µs to hours. Pharos learns to steer simulations across the barrier. In stochastic optimal control the best steering is set by the committor, the probability of completing the transition, so Pharos learns both, and with them mechanisms and rates.

Yuanqi Du @YuanqiD · 3/ why it is hard + the committor 273
Why is it hard? Barriers many times the thermal energy make escape exponentially slow; MD steps are femtoseconds. The committor q captures a transition: the probability of reaching the product first. It is central to the rate, all reaction statistics and the mechanism. 🧭
Yuanqi Du @YuanqiD · 4/ stochastic optimal control 271
Our approach: stochastic optimal control 🎛️. Add a learned steering force; the optimal one, carrying every trajectory across, is set by the committor. So Pharos runs a loop: q steers simulations into the transition region, and those paths sharpen q, which steers better.
