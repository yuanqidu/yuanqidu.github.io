About the symposium

Two perspectives.
One molecular world.

Data-driven machine learning is expanding what we can predict and design. Physics-driven methods bring the constraints, energy models, and statistical mechanics that help us understand why molecular systems behave as they do.

This symposium brings these communities into conversation: where do learned priors and physical models complement each other, and what balance should we strike when data are scarce? Together, we will explore their intersection across structure, thermodynamics, kinetics, and sampling, alongside the emerging role of large language models.

Three connected scientific themes

01 / STRUCTURE

Structure & molecular design

Where structure prediction meets energy models: connecting learned priors with physical energy functions in co-folding and co-design.

02 / DYNAMICS

Thermodynamics & kinetics

How machine learning changes the calculation of free energies, reaction rates, and other molecular properties, especially in data-scarce settings.

03 / EXPLORATION

Sampling molecular systems

Finding the right balance between physics-driven exploration and data-driven proposals to reach the states and transitions that matter.
